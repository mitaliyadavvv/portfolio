SERVICE DESIGN • GOVTECH • 2026 UX INDIA 2026 GOLD TROPHY

Simplifying the grueling administrative maze of death for grieving families.

When a family member passes away in India, surviving kin must navigate up to 14 disconnected government and financial entities during acute grief. Prayan transforms bureaucratic fragmentation into an empathetic, single-touchpoint transition service—awarded the Best Student Innovation Gold Award at UX India 2026.

01. FIELD INSIGHTS

Bureaucracy does not pause for grief.

Neuroscience confirms that acute bereavement induces severe short-term memory impairment, cognitive disorientation, and executive function degradation.

Yet, within 72 hours of a bereavement in India, families are subjected to a bewildering maze: municipal corporators for death certificates, banks for KYC freeze, insurance adjusters, EPF pension filings, and property transfer notices.

ETHNOGRAPHIC INTERVIEW

"I was standing in line at the municipal ward office on Day 3, clutching my father's original hospital death slip. The clerk scolded me for not having 6 photocopies attested by a gazetted officer. I broke down on the floor."

02. SERVICE ARCHITECTURE

One digital notification federated across the entire civic ecosystem.

Instead of putting the burden of reconciliation on grieving individuals, Prayan introduces an event-driven civic backbone: the verified hospital death entry securely cascades to civic and financial registries.

CANDID REFLECTION

Design as an act of public stewardship.

When designing for grief, every redundant text field is an act of cruelty, and every ambiguous status notification feels like a betrayal.

This project permanently altered how I understand product design: our highest duty as designers is not to make interfaces "engaging" or to maximize time spent on screen, but to build services that step into people's lives with quiet dignity, solve their hardest problems, and get out of their way.

Prayan taught me that systems thinking only matters when it is rooted in deep, unflinching empathy for human vulnerability.
