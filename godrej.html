GODREJ & BOYCE • FIELD ENTERPRISE MOBILITY • 2024

Cutting CRM lead friction by 52% for 2,400+ industrial dealer reps.

Industrial sales representatives for Godrej & Boyce operate in noisy fabrication plants and remote warehouses where connectivity is volatile. The legacy enterprise mobile app suffered from an unusable 9-step form that led to 68% field abandonment. We redesigned the mobile experience with one-thumb ergonomics, smart defaults, voice note capture, and guaranteed offline queueing.

Role Mobile Product Design Intern
Team 1 Design Lead, 2 Android Eng, 1 PM
Timeline 3 Months (Research to Pilot Rollout)
Key Metric 52% Fewer Taps • 15h Saved / Mo

01. FIELD CONTEXT

The ergonomic constraints of the factory floor.

Software designers often work comfortably on 32-inch monitors in quiet air-conditioned studios. But an industrial sales representative lives in high decibels, glaring midday sunlight, and dusty environments.

Field reps routinely hold a heavy product catalogue or measuring tape in one hand, forcing all smartphone operations to be executed with a single thumb.

SHADOWING DISCOVERY

Reps bypassed the corporate CRM app completely during client meetings. They scribbled notes on torn paper napkins, then spent 3 hours every Sunday night manually back-filling fake data into the system just to satisfy management quotas.

02. ERGONOMIC REDESIGN

Architecting for the natural arc of the human thumb.

We eliminated nested menus, top-appbar buttons, and modal dialogs. Every essential operation was collapsed into a persistent, thumb-reachable bottom sheet with tactile swipe gestures and smart defaults.

04. SYSTEM RESILIENCE

Zero lost leads: Resilient offline queueing.

In subterranean machine shops and sheet-metal warehouses, cellular reception drops to zero. We designed an offline-first SQLite state machine that records leads instantly and syncs opportunistically when the device senses signal.

CANDID REFLECTION

Designing for workers, not just for corporate dashboards.

Enterprise software often fails because it is procured by executives who care about reporting compliance, but operated by overworked individuals in challenging physical conditions.

Our breakthrough came when we stopped asking "What data does headquarters want?" and instead asked "What is the absolute minimum effort required for this sales rep to get back to their real job?"

By prioritizing single-thumb biomechanics and guaranteed offline reliability, we created an interface that field workers actively enjoyed using, which in turn gave headquarters higher data fidelity than ever before.
